Definitions: printed form and form used by the proofs

The Lean modules Stafford38.Paper state the definitions of the paper as printed. Some of these are the definitions the Lean proofs work with. Others are stated separately and connected, by a Lean theorem, to a different definition that the proofs use. That definition lives in the working modules, the other modules of the project. This page lists which case applies to each notion: 12 are used as printed, 7 are other names, 4 are Mathlib's, for 16 an equivalence is proved and for 1 an implication.

Notation: k is the ground field of characteristic zero, K an algebraically closed field containing it, over which the geometry of Section 5 takes place. A point is a tuple of coordinates in K. The stand-alone statements are the versions of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 6.3 written with Mathlib alone, against which the proofs are checked independently (the cards "Challenge comparison" of the proof comparison). The Lean sources are those of commit 1029fdb of the formal repository, with Mathlib at commit c55e6e7.

  • as printed: The Lean definition is the printed one, and the statements and proofs use it as it stands. A theorem listed in such a row relates it to a further form that single steps use.
  • another name: The printed name abbreviates a definition of the working modules. Both are the same object by definition; no theorem is needed.
  • Mathlib: The printed notion is Mathlib's. Where the paper describes it, a Lean theorem states that description as a property of Mathlib's notion.
  • equivalence proved: The printed definition is written out on its own, the proofs work with a different definition, and Lean theorems prove that the two agree.
  • implication proved: As before, but Lean proves only the direction the proofs need: the printed condition implies the one the proofs use.

Under each connecting theorem the page says whether a later proof uses it. Lean stores, for every proof, the declarations it refers to; following these references from Theorem 1.1 (Stafford38.Paper.theorem_1_1) and from Corollary 6.3 gives everything their proofs depend on. A connecting theorem outside this set is still proved; it documents that the printed form agrees, and no later argument rests on it.

Geometry: where the definitions of Section 5 live

Section 5 is the geometric part of the proof. Its Lean definitions sit in three layers. General notions come from Mathlib. The notions specific to this proof are defined in the project's own geometry modules, Stafford38.Geometry. The modules Stafford38.Paper.Section5 restate them as printed and prove the statements of the paper. The separate library AlgebraicAnalysis, on which the project also depends, contributes Ore extensions and filtered modules to Sections 3 and 4 (25 of its modules are used by the proof of Theorem 1.1) and no geometry.

Core geometric definitions

All are sets or subspaces given by explicit conditions on points y ∈ K^n and covectors ξ ∈ (K^n)*. An irreducible closed set Y enters through its prime ideal 𝔭 = I(Y), a point of Spec K[x]. No schemes or sheaves are involved beyond Mathlib's prime spectrum.
