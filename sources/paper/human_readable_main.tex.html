53 \begin{abstract}
54 We prove that over a Weyl algebra over a field of characteristic zero $A_n(k)$, every nonzero $d\in A_n(k)$ admits $F,R,S\in A_n(k)$ such that
55 $1=dR+FdS$, a conjecture Stafford proposed in 1978. As a consequence, every finitely generated torsion right $A_n(k)$-module is cyclic.
56 The proof maps $d$, by a symplectic change of variables, to a normal form $\tilde d$
57 and shows that the cyclic module $Q=A_n(k)/(\tilde dA_n(k)+x_1^N\tilde dA_n(k))$ vanishes.
58 We first show that right multiplication by $x_1$ is surjective on $Q$. A comparison of lengths of localized modules
59 over the associated graded ring then gives $\operatorname{gr}Q=x_1\operatorname{gr}Q$,
60 so that the characteristic variety of $Q$ is disjoint from the hyperplane
61 $\{x_1=0\}$. Finally, Gabber's involutivity theorem together with an argument about conormal directions at infinity from algebraic geometry shows that such a characteristic variety must be empty and therefore, $Q = 0$.
62 \end{abstract}

67 \section{Introduction}
68 
69 Let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero, and let
70 \[ A_n(k)=k\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n,\partial_1,\ldots,\partial_n\rangle\]
71 be the $n$th Weyl algebra, with $[\partial_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij}$ and
72 $[\partial_i,\partial_j]=[x_i,x_j]=0$.
73 
74 Stafford's Conjecture~3.8 in \cite{Stafford1978} asks whether every nonzero $d\in A_n(k)$ admits $F\in A_n(k)$ with $A_n(k)=dA_n(k)+FdA_n(k)$
75 \cite[p.~438]{Stafford1978}. Stafford shows that this is a formulation that implies stable cyclicity for all finitely generated torsion right Weyl-algebra modules
76 \cite[pp.~437--438]{Stafford1978}. 
77 We prove Stafford's conjecture:
78 \begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
79 For every field of characteristic zero $k$, every $n\geq0$, and every
80 nonzero $d\in A_n(k)$, there exists an $F\in A_n(k)$ such that
81 \[ A_n(k)=dA_n(k)+FdA_n(k),\]
82 or equivalenty, there exist $F, S, R\in A_n(k)$ such that
83 \[ 1=dR+FdS.\]
84 Moreover, if $n\ge1$, one may choose $F=\Phi_g^{-1}(x_1)^{\deg_Bd}$ for a linear
85 $\omega$-preserving automorphism $g$ as in Section~\ref{sec:monic}. 
86 \end{theorem}

100 \textit{Proof sketch:}
101 We state some preliminaries in section \ref{sec:setting}, then start in section \ref{sec:monic} by defining a map $\Phi_g$ sending any chosen $d$ of Bernstein degree $N$ to a monic normal form $\tilde d$ with order symbol $P$ using Bernstein and order filtrations of $A_n(k)$. For $\tilde d$, we prove in sections \ref{sec:euler}-\ref{sec:proof} that the module $Q = A_n(k)/(\tilde d A_n(k) + x_1^N\tilde d A_n(k)) = 0$, and find the final form of Theorem \ref{thm:main} by applying the inverse map $\Phi_g^{-1}$ to $\tilde d A_n(k) + x_1^N\tilde d A_n(k).$ The main components of the proof are the following: in section \ref{sec:euler}, we show that $Q = Qx_1$ using the extended euclidean algorithm, and $\gr Q = x_1\gr Q,$ through two contradictory length estimations of localized modules. In section \ref{sec:conormal-review} using mainly results from algebraic geometry, we show that a set $W$ fulfilling certain criteria (Zariski closed over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, fibre-conical, involutive, and there exists a polynomial with $P(1,0\dots,0)\neq 0$ vanishing on $W$, and $W$ does not contain points with $x_1 = 0$) must always be empty (Theorem \ref{thm:coisotropic-exclusion}). Finally, we show in section \ref{sec:proof} that by defining $W$ to be the characteristic variety of the closed-field pendant to $Q$, the results of section \ref{sec:euler} as well as the definition of $P$ in section \ref{sec:monic} ensure that $W$ fulfills all criteria and must thus be empty. We conclude that therefore, also $Q$ itself must be zero, from which Theorem \ref{thm:main} follows directly. Lastly, we provide the proof by Stafford \cite{Stafford1978} that hence, every finitely generated torsion module is stably cyclic (Corollary \ref{cor:cyclic}).

104 \subsection{Related literature}
105 Dixmier showed that over the first Weyl algebra $A_1(k)$ with $k$ having characteristic zero, any right ideal can be generated by at most two elements \cite{dixmier}. In a seminal paper in 1978, Stafford extended this result to all $A_n(k)$ and moreover showed, that any finitely generated left torsion $A_n(k)$-module is isomorphic to a right ideal, and as such can also be (stably) generated be at most two elements \cite[Theorem 3.7]{Stafford1978}. 
106 Hillebrand and Schmale \cite{HillebrandSchmale2001}, as well as Leykin \cite{Leykin2004} give an algorithm to find the two generators of left ideals, also valid for rational coefficient algebras $B_n(k)$. Caro and Lefcovitz \cite{CaroLevcovitz2010} generalize the result for both left and right ideals to formal and to convergent Laurent series, and and Quadrat and Robertz \cite{QuadratRobertz2014} provide a \textit{constructive} proof method to calculate the two generators of torsion modules over the Weyl algebras, as well as rings with rational coefficients, formal or convergent Laurent series, and, for ordinary differential operators with formal or locally convergent power series coefficients. 
107 
108 In the same paper, Stafford states the Conjecture 3.8, which is Theorem \ref{thm:main}, and shows that this conjecture implies that any finitely generated right torsion module can be stably generated by one element, i.e. is stably cyclic (Corollary \ref{cor:cyclic}). He shows that cyclicity is true if $n = 1$ (since $A_1(k)$ has the restricted minimum condition which makes torsion modules Artinian and he proves that Artinian modules are cyclic) and also states that the conjecture is true if $d$ is monomial, but provides, as far as the authors are aware, no proof of this. This case is also contained in our proof.
109 For \textit{holonomic} modules over "simple rings such that they do not have finite length over themselves as left modules" (which all Weyl algebras are), Leykin \cite{Leykin2004} proved cyclicity constructively, and attributed the proof method to Stafford. However, torsion modules need not be holonomic: for $n\ge2$, Stafford constructed quotients $A_n(k)/dA_n(k)$ of Gelfand--Kirillov dimension $2n-1$ as counterexamples  \cite{StaffordNonholonomic1985}, so the question about cyclicity remained open for non-holonomic torsion modules. A survey by Bellamy in 2026 \cite{Bellamy2026} states this explicitly (Conjecture 3.4). 
110 
111 We base our proof on the Weyl algebra and on filtrations and characteristic
112 varieties of $A_n(k)$-modules as treated in the textbooks
113 \cite{coutinho,Bjork,HTT,MR,SchnellNotes}; on the involutivity of
114 the characteristic variety due to Gabber \cite{Gabber1981,Gabber1982,SinghKumar2014}; on commutative algebra, in particular localization, length, valuation rings, Kähler differentials and completions
115 \cite{atiyah,matsumura,Stacks}, together with field theory and
116 bilinear forms \cite{lang} and spectral sequences \cite{weibel}; and on
117 algebraic geometry, mostly based on \cite{hartshorne}.

163 \begin{definition}[{[Def.~2.6 and ~2.9 \cite{SchnellNotes}]}]\label{def:filtration}
164 A filtration $F_\bullet$ on $A_n(k)$ is a sequence of linear subspaces
165 \[\{0\} = F_{-1} \subseteq F_0 \subseteq F_1\subseteq...\subseteq A_n(k),\]
166 such that $F_i\cdot F_j \subseteq F_{i+j}$ and $A_n(k) = \bigcup F_j.$
167 The associated graded algebra, also called symbol ring, is defined as
168 \[\mathrm{gr}^F A_n(k) = \bigoplus_{j = 0}^\infty F_j/F_{j-1}.\]
169 Let $a\neq 0 \in A_n(k)$ and let $j$ be the smallest integer, such that $a \in F_j$, called a \emph{filtration degree} $\deg_F a$. The class of $a$ in $F_j/F_{j-1}$ is called its \emph{symbol} $\sigma_j(a)$. A symbol, being the class of an element in a single graded piece, is automatically homogeneous of the corresponding
170 degree, i.e.\ contains only terms of that total degree.
171 \end{definition}
172 
173 In the proof, we use two filtrations of $A_n(k)$ (for more details, see e.g. \cite[Chapter 7]{coutinho})
174 \begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
175 \item The \emph{Bernstein filtration} $B_\bullet$ assigns degree $1$ to every
176   $x_i$ and every $\partial_i$. The Bernstein degree of $d$ is
177   written $\deg_B d$.
178 \item The \emph{order filtration} $F_\bullet$ assigns degree $0$ to every $x_i$
179   and degree $1$ to every $\partial_i$; $F_m$ is spanned by the monomials
180   $x^\alpha\partial^\beta$ with $|\beta|\le m$.  %We write $\sigma_m(a)$ for the order-$m$ principal symbol.
181 \end{itemize}

189 \begin{definition}[{\cite[Ch. 8.3]{coutinho}}]\label{def:good-filtration}
190 A filtration of a right $A_n$-module $M$ compatible with a filtration $F_\bullet$
191 is an exhaustive increasing chain of $k$-subspaces $M_\bullet$ with
192 $M_iF_j\subseteq M_{i+j}$ and $M_i=0$ for $i<0$. The associated graded module is $\operatorname{gr}M=\bigoplus_iM_i/M_{i-1}$; it is a graded module over $\operatorname{gr}_FA_n(k)$ via the maps
193 $M_i/M_{i-1}\times F_j/F_{j-1}\to M_{i+j}/M_{i+j-1}$, $(\bar m,\bar a)\mapsto\overline{ma}$,
194 which are well defined because $M_{i-1}F_j\subseteq M_{i+j-1}$ and $M_iF_{j-1}\subseteq M_{i+j-1}$.
195 It is \emph{good} if the associated graded module is finitely generated over $\gr^FA_n$.
196 \end{definition}
197 As an example, the filtration $M_i:=qF_i$ of a cyclic module over $A_n$ induced by its generator $q$ is good, since $\gr Q$ is generated by the element $\bar{q}$ denoting the class of $q$ in $Q_0/Q_{-1}.$

202 \begin{definition}\label{def:cotangent}
203 Let $X=\mathbb A^n_k$ be affine $n$-space over $k$ with coordinates
204 $x_1,\dots,x_n$. Since $X$ has a global coordinate frame, the \emph{cotangent
205 bundle} of $X$ is
206 \[ T^*X=X\times(k^n)^*.\]
207 We write $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n$ for the coordinates on $(k^n)^*$ dual to $x_1,\dots,x_n$,
208 so that a covector is $\sum_i\xi_i\,dx_i$. The coordinate ring of $T^*X$ is
209 $k[x,\xi]=\gr A_n(k)$, and we regard $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[x]$ and
210 $T^*X=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,\xi]$ as sets of prime ideals \cite[Tag 00DZ]{Stacks} (Definition~\ref{def:spec}).
211 If $k$ is algebraically closed, the closed points are the pairs $(y,\xi)$ with
212 $y\in k^n$ a point and $\xi$ a covector at $y$ (Theorem \ref{thm:nss}(a)). The \emph{projection} is
213 $\pi\colon T^*X\to X$, $(y,\xi)\mapsto y$, and for $y\in X$ the \emph{fibre} over $y$
214 is $T_y^*X=\pi^{-1}(y)\cong(k^n)^*$.
215 \end{definition}

220 \begin{definition}\label{def:spec}
221 Let $S$ be a commutative ring. The \emph{spectrum} $\operatorname{Spec}S$ is the set
222 of all prime ideals of $S$. We say that $f\in S$ vanishes at the point
223 $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}S$ if $f\in\mathfrak p$. The \emph{vanishing locus}
224 of an ideal $J\subseteq S$ is
225 \[ V(J)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}S:\ \mathfrak p\supseteq J\},\]
226 the set of points at which all elements of $J$ vanish; these sets are the (Zariski) closed sets
227 of the Zariski topology on $\operatorname{Spec}S$. For a field $K$ and an ideal $J\subseteq K[z_1,\dots,z_N]$ we write
228 \[V_K(J)=\{a\in K^N : f(a)=0\ \forall f\in J\}\]
229 for its (classical) zero set, and for $S\subseteq K^N$ we write
230 $I(S)=\{f\in K[z]: f(a)=0\ \forall a\in S\}$ for the \emph{vanishing ideal} of $S$.
231 The sets $V_K(J)$ are the closed sets of the Zariski topology on $K^N$.
232 Since $f\in\mathfrak m_a:=(z_1-a_1,\dots,z_N-a_N)$ iff $f(a)=0$, we have
233 $a\in V_K(J)$ iff $\mathfrak m_a\in V(J)$.
234 \end{definition}

251 \begin{definition}[{\cite[Ch. E.1]{HTT}}]\label{def:symplectic}
252 A bilinear form $\omega$ on a $k$-vector space is \emph{symplectic} if it is
253 antisymmetric and nondegenerate. The \emph{standard symplectic form} on $k^{2n}$
254 with basis $x_1,\dots,x_n,\partial_1,\dots,\partial_n$ is determined by
255 \[ \omega(x_i,\partial_j)=\delta_{ij},\qquad
256  \omega(x_i,x_j)=\omega(\partial_i,\partial_j)=0,\]
257 analogously to the Weyl relations. Under the correspondence $\partial_i\leftrightarrow\xi_i$,
258 on $T^*X$ (Definition~\ref{def:cotangent}) it takes the familiar form
259 $\omega=\sum_{i=1}^n dx_i\wedge d\xi_i$.
260 The form is \emph{nondegenerate} if $\omega(u,\cdot)=0$ implies $u=0$. For a subspace $U$,
261 $U^\perp=\{v:\omega(u,v)=0\ \forall u\in U\}$. A \emph{symplectic basis} is a basis
262 $e_1,\dots,e_n,f_1,\dots,f_n$ with $\omega(e_i,f_j)=\delta_{ij}$ and
263 $\omega(e_i,e_j)=\omega(f_i,f_j)=0$. The \emph{Gram matrix} of $\omega$ in a basis
264 $(b_i)$ is $J=(\omega(b_i,b_j))_{ij}$.
265 \end{definition}

318 \section{Mapping to a normal form}\label{sec:monic}
319 Given a fixed nonzero $d \in A_n(k)$ of Bernstein degree $N$, our first goal is to find a map to replace $d$ by a normal form $\tilde{d}$ that is monic in $p$, i.e. of the form $\tilde d=p^N+\sum_{j<N}p^ja_j$ with coefficients $a_j$ in $C$. \\
320 
321 If $N=0$, then $d \in B_0 = k$ is a nonzero scalar, hence a unit, and $1 = dR+FdS$ holds trivially with $F=1$, $R=d^{-1}$, $S=0$. We therefore assume $N\geq1$.

324 We want to construct a well-behaved automorphism of $\gr^BA_n(k)=k[x,\xi]$
325 from simpler linear algebra. We proceed in three stages:
326 define an automorphism of the vector space $\mathbb V=k^{2n}$, extend it to
327 an automorphism of $A_n(k)$ itself, and observe the induced action this
328 extension leaves on the associated graded ring.
329 
330 We can identify $\mathbb V = k^{2n}$ with the Bernstein-degree-one part $B_1/B_0$ of
331 $A_n(k)$, i.e.\ linear combinations of $x_1,\ldots,x_n,\partial_1,\ldots,
332 \partial_n$; the coordinates of a vector are then the coefficients of
333 these generators. By this identification, $\mathbb V$ carries the standard symplectic form $\omega$ (Def. \ref{def:symplectic}).

335 \begin{lemma}\label{lem:sympcompl}
336 Let $(\mathbb V,\omega)$ be a symplectic $k$-space of dimension $2n$ and $0\neq v\in \mathbb V$.
337 Then $v$ is the first vector of a symplectic basis of $\mathbb V$; consequently, there
338 exists a $k$-linear automorphism $g$ preserving $\omega$ (i.e. $\omega(gu,gv)=\omega(u,v)$ for all
339 $u,v\in\mathbb V$.) with $g(e)=v$ for any prescribed nonzero vector $e$.
340 \end{lemma}
341 
342 \begin{proof}
343 Since $\omega$ is nondegenerate and $v\neq0$, the functional $\omega(v,-)$
344 is not identically zero, so there is $w'\in\mathbb V$ with
345 $\omega(v,w')=c\neq0$; setting $w:=w'/c$ gives $\omega(v,w)=1$.
346 
347 Let $U=\operatorname{span}(v,w)$. For $0\neq av+bw\in U$: if $a\neq0$,
348 $\omega(av+bw,w)=a\neq0$; if $b\neq0$, $\omega(av+bw,v)=-b\neq0$. Either
349 way $av+bw$ pairs nontrivially with something in $U$, so $\omega|_U$ is
350 nondegenerate.
351 
352 Since $\omega|_U$ is nondegenerate, $U\cap U^\perp=0$, and as
353 $\dim U+\dim U^\perp=2n = \dim\mathbb V$ \cite[Prop. 11.2.1]{coutinho} for a nondegenerate form, this gives
354 $\mathbb V=U\oplus U^\perp$. If $u\in U^\perp$ pairs to zero with all of
355 $U^\perp$, it also pairs to zero with $U$ (as $u\in U^\perp$), hence with
356 all of $\mathbb V$, forcing $u=0$; so $\omega|_{U^\perp}$ is nondegenerate
357 too.
358 
359 By induction on $n$ (trivial for $n=1$, where $U=\mathbb V$), a
360 symplectic basis $e_2,\ldots,f_n$ of $U^\perp$ exists \cite[XV.8.1 and XV.8.2]{lang}; setting $e_1:=v$,
361 $f_1:=w$ gives a symplectic basis of $\mathbb V=U\oplus U^\perp$, since
362 cross-pairings between $U$ and $U^\perp$ vanish by definition of
363 $U^\perp$.
364 
365 Consequently: given nonzero $v,e\in\mathbb V$, we can complete both vectors to a symplectic basis as above. We then define the linear map $g$ to send $e$ to $v$, and each subsequent $e$-basis vector to the corresponding $v$-basis vector, thus preserving all pairwise values of $\omega$ on basis vectors. Through the bilinearity of $\omega$, $g$ then preserves $\omega$ on all of $\mathbb V$; and $g(e)=v$.
366 \end{proof}

384 \begin{proposition}\label{prop:monic}
385 Let $0\neq d\in A_n(k)$ with $N:=\deg_B d\ge1$. There exists an automorphism $\Phi_g$ of $A_n(k)$ and
386 $c\in k^{\times}$ such that
387 \begin{equation}
388   \tilde d:=c^{-1}\Phi_g(d)=p^{\,N}+\sum_{j<N}p^{\,j}a_j, 
389   \qquad a_j\in C,\quad \deg_B a_j\le N-j\label{eq:monic-d}

392 \end{proposition}
393 
394 \begin{proof}
395 Let $s\in k[x,\xi]$ be the Bernstein principal symbol of $d$, a nonzero
396 homogeneous polynomial of degree $N$ on $\mathbb V$. A characteristic-zero field is
397 infinite, so every nonzero polynomial has a nonvanishing value: we choose $v\in \mathbb V$
398 with $s(v)\neq0$. Because $s$ is homogeneous of degree $N\geq 1$, $s(0)$ must vanish, therefore $v$ is nonzero. 
399 Let $e_p\in\mathbb V$ be the standard basis vector corresponding to $p$.
400 By Lemma~\ref{lem:sympcompl} there is an $\omega$-preserving automorphism
401 $h$ of $\mathbb V$ with $h(e_p)=v$.
402 If $h$ preserves $\omega$, then $h^{\mathsf T}Jh=J$, where $J$ is the Gram matrix
403 of $\omega$. In particular $h$ is invertible, and inverting gives
404 $h^{-1}J^{-1}(h^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=J^{-1}$, i.e.\ $J^{-1}=hJ^{-1}h^{\mathsf T}$.
405 Since $J^2=-\mathrm{Id}$, i.e.\ $J^{-1}=-J$, this is $hJh^{\mathsf T}=J$, so
406 $g:=h^{\mathsf T}$  again preserves $\omega$. Let $\Phi_g$ be the
407 corresponding automorphism of $A_n(k)$, extending $u\mapsto g(u)$ on
408 generators.
409 Let $M$ be the matrix whose rows are the images of the generators, i.e.\
410 $g(u_i)=\sum_j M_{ij}u_j$. Since $\Phi_g$ sends each generator to a
411 degree-one element, it maps $B_m$ into $B_m$ for all $m$, and since
412 Bernstein symbols are multiplicative, a standard monomial of degree $N$ is
413 mapped to an element of $B_N$ whose class modulo $B_{N-1}$ is obtained by
414 substituting $u_i\mapsto\sum_j M_{ij}u_j$ in the corresponding commutative
415 monomial. Hence $\Phi_g(d)\equiv s(Mu)\bmod B_{N-1}$. As $M$ is invertible,
416 $s(Mu)$ is again a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree $N$, so
417 $\deg_B\Phi_g(d)=N$ and the Bernstein symbol of $\Phi_g(d)$ is the
418 function $s_g\colon a\mapsto s(Ma)$. Since the rows of $M$ are the coordinate vectors of the $g(u_i)$, the matrix of
419 $g$ is $M^T$; as $g=h^T$, this gives $M=h$, i.e.\ $Me_j=h(e_j)$. Hence the
420 value of $s_g$ at $e_p$ is
421 \[ s_g(e_p) = s(Me_p)=s(h(e_p))=s(v)\neq0 .\]
422 Set $c:=s(v)$. Since $c$ is a nonzero scalar, it is a central unit of
423 $A_n(k)$, and $\tilde d:=c^{-1}\Phi_g(d)$ satisfies $\deg_B\tilde d=N$ and
424 $\sigma_N^B(\tilde d)(e_p)=1$.
425 Using the normal form $A_n(k)=\bigoplus_j p^{\,j}C$, we can write
426 $\tilde d=\sum_{j = 0}^N p^{\,j}a_j$ with $a_j\in C$. The Bernstein filtration is
427 compatible with this decomposition, so
428 $N=\deg_B\tilde d=\max_j\,(j+\deg_B a_j)$, and we have $\deg_B a_j\le N-j$ for
429 all $j$; in particular $a_j=0$ for $j>N$ and $a_N\in C\cap B_0=k$. 
430 The symbol is
431 \[
432   \sigma_N^B(\tilde d)=\sum_{j+\deg_B a_j=N}\sigma_N^B(p)^{\,j}\,\sigma_N^B(a_j),
433 \]
434 For $j<N$ the factors $\sigma_N^B(a_j)$ must have a positive degree and must therefore be non-constant in a coordinate other than $p$, so they vanish at $e_p$. Because we defined $\tilde d$ to be $c^{-1}\Phi_g(d)$ , evaluating at $e_p$ gives $a_N=\sigma_N^B(\tilde d)(e_p)=c^{-1}\sigma_N^B(\Phi_g(d))(e_p) = c^{-1}s_g(e_p) = c^{-1}s(v)=1$, and therefore
435 \[ \tilde d=p^{\,N}+\sum_{j<N}p^{\,j}a_j,\qquad a_j\in C,\quad
436   \deg_B a_j\le N-j. \]
437 \end{proof}

476 Let $\tilde d=p^N+\sum_{j<N}p^{\,j}a_j$ with $a_j\in C$, $\deg_Ba_j\le N-j$,

620 \begin{definition}[{\cite{Stacks}[Tag 00CM]}] \label{def:localization}
621 Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $S\subseteq R$ a multiplicative subset
622 (i.e.\ $1\in S$ and $S$ is closed under products). The \emph{localization}
623 $S^{-1}R$ is the ring of fractions $a/s$ ($a\in R$, $s\in S$), where
624 \[ \frac{a}{s}=\frac{a'}{s'}\iff t(s'a-sa')=0\ \text{ for some }t\in S,\]
625 with the usual addition and multiplication of fractions. For an $R$-module
626 $N$, the \emph{localization} $S^{-1}N$ is the $S^{-1}R$-module of fractions
627 $y/s$ ($y\in N$, $s\in S$), with $y/s=y'/s'$ iff $t(s'y-sy')=0$ for some
628 $t\in S$. For a prime $\mathfrak p\subset R$ we write
629 $R_{\mathfrak p}=S^{-1}R$ and $N_{\mathfrak p}=S^{-1}N$ for
630 $S=R\setminus\mathfrak p$. Localization is exact: it sends exact sequences
631 of $R$-modules to exact sequences of $S^{-1}R$-modules \cite[Tag 00CS]{Stacks}.
632 \end{definition}

635     \[\Supp_R(M) = \{\mathfrak{p} \in \Spec(R) | M_{\mathfrak{p}} \neq 0\}.\]
636 \end{definition}

759 For every minimal prime $\mathfrak q$ in $\operatorname{Supp}_T U$,

762  \le \ell_{T_{\mathfrak q}}(V_{\mathfrak q}).

767 To prove the inequality we set up a spectral sequence measuring how $\ker(f)$ and
768 $\operatorname{coker}(f)$ acting on $Q$ itself compare, order by order, to $V$ and
769 $U$, acting on $\gr Q$.
770 
771 Consider the filtered complex $Q\xrightarrow{f}Q$, with decreasing filtration $G^p=F_{-p}Q$, where $F$ is
772 the order filtration. Since ${x_1}$ has order $0$, $f(G^p)\subseteq G^p$ for every
773 $p$; the induced map on $G^p/G^{p+1}$, summed over $p$, is exactly the action of
774 ${x_1}$ on $M=\operatorname{gr}Q$. This is the crudest approximation to $\ker_Q(f)$ and
775 $\operatorname{coker}_Q(f)$: it correctly identifies $V,U$ as approximations, but it
776 does not yet see elements that vanish to higher order than this naive count
777 predicts. The spectral sequence refines this approximation in successive stages,
778 called \emph{pages} (see also \cite[Construction 5.4.6]{weibel}): for each $r\ge0$, the source and target pages are
779 \[
780  A_r=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb Z}\frac{G^p\cap f^{-1}(G^{p+r})}{G^{p+1}\cap f^{-1}(G^{p+r})},
781  \qquad
782  B_r=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb Z}\frac{G^p}{(G^p\cap f(G^{p-r+1}))+G^{p+1}},
783 \]
784 where the degree-$p$ summand of $A_r$ isolates elements of $G^p$ whose image under
785 $f$ drops $r$ extra filtration levels (candidates for the kernel, accurate to
786 order $r$), and the degree-$p$ summand of $B_r$ isolates the part of $G^p$ not yet
787 hit by $f$ from $r-1$ levels shallower.
788 
789 Applying $f$ to a representative gives a well-defined map $d_r:A_r\to B_r$,
790 shifting the summand index by $r$; one checks directly from these definitions that
791 \[ A_0=B_0=M,\qquad d_0={x_1},\qquad A_{r+1}=\ker d_r,\qquad B_{r+1}=\operatorname{coker}d_r,\]
792 so that each page is obtained from the previous one by taking kernel and cokernel
793 of $d_r$, exactly as in the construction of a spectral sequence from a filtered
794 complex.

796 These pages carry a $T$-module structure. The pages $A_r,B_r$ are
797 subquotients of $Q$, which a priori carries an action of the transverse Weyl
798 algebra $A_{n-1}(k)$ by right multiplication. This action commutes with
799 $f$, since the transverse algebra commutes with $x_1$, so the maps
800 $d_r$ are compatible with it. Two transverse elements commute only up to a
801 commutator, which lowers the order filtration by one. On the page
802 quotients such lower-order terms vanish, so the induced actions commute, and
803 the commutative ring $T$ acts on every $A_r$ and $B_r$, via the symbols of
804 the transverse generators. The maps $d_r$ and the successor-page maps are
805 $T$-linear. In particular, this means that localizing at $T\setminus\mathfrak q$ and
806 measuring lengths over $T_{\mathfrak q}$ is a well-defined action for the pages.

815 have finite length as well.

903 additivity along $0\to\ker \varphi\to L\to \varphi(L)\to0$ and $0\to \varphi(L)\to L\to L/\varphi(L)\to0$ gives

906 \[\ell(K_{\infty}/{x_1}K_{\infty})=\ell(V_{\mathfrak q}).\]

908 On the other hand, the natural surjection $M_{\mathfrak q}/({x_1}M_{\mathfrak q})\to (M_{\mathfrak q}/K_{\infty})/({x_1}(M_{\mathfrak q}/K_{\infty}))$ has

911  \tilde E/({x_1}\tilde E)\longrightarrow0,\]
912 so by additivity and using $\ell(K_{\infty}/({x_1}K_{\infty}))=\ell(V_{\mathfrak q})$,
913 \[\ell(U_{\mathfrak q}) = \ell(V_{\mathfrak q}) + \ell\bigl(\tilde E/({x_1}\tilde E)\bigr).\]

919 $0\to (K_{\infty})_{\mathfrak p'}\to (M_{\mathfrak q})_{\mathfrak p'}\to \tilde E_{\mathfrak p'}\to0$ we get
920 $\tilde E_{\mathfrak p'}\cong (M_{\mathfrak q})_{\mathfrak p'}\ne0$, so $\mathfrak p'$ lies in the support of $M_{\mathfrak q}/K_{\infty}$ in $k[x,\xi]_{\mathfrak q}$.
921 Since support is closed under passing to larger primes \cite[Tag 00L2]{Stacks} and
922 $\mathfrak p'\subseteq\mathfrak P$, also $\mathfrak P\in\operatorname{Supp}_{k[x,\xi]_{\mathfrak q}}\tilde E$, i.e.
923 $\tilde E_{\mathfrak P}\ne0$.

926 ${x_1}$, we have ${x_1}\in\mathfrak P$. If $({x_1}\tilde E_{\mathfrak P})=\tilde E_{\mathfrak P}$, then as

933 \end{proof}

956 This section uses mainly vocabulary and concepts from algebraic geometry. We will define the most important quantities used here (for a comprehensive overview of the concepts of algebraic geometry, we refer to textbooks such as \cite{hartshorne}), then state the two main results. Theorem~\ref{thm:asymptotic-conormal-formal}
957 shows that for a closed irreducible subvariety $Y\subseteq X$ not meeting the hyperplane
958 $\{x_1=0\}$, the covector $dx_1$ is an asymptotic conormal direction of $Y$.
959 Theorem~\ref{thm:coisotropic-exclusion} uses it to show that no nonempty closed,
960 fibre-conical, involutive subset $W\subseteq T^*X$ whose projection to $X$ does not meet the hyperplane $\{x_1 = 0\}$ 
961 can be contained in the zero set of a polynomial $P(\xi)$ with $P(1,0,\dots,0)\neq0$.
962 We prove the first theorem in Subsection~\ref{sub:asym}, with the lemmas stated and

965 \subsection{Preliminaries and definitions from algebraic geometry}\label{sub:def_geom}
966 Throughout this section $K$ denotes an algebraically closed field of characteristic
967 zero. We apply the same notation as in Definition \ref{def:cotangent} with $k=K$ and, by Theorem~\ref{thm:nss}, identify $X=K^n$ with the closed points of
968 $\mathbb A^n_K$ and $T^*X=K^n\times(K^n)^*$ with the closed points of
969 $\operatorname{Spec}K[x,\xi]$, with \emph{affine coordinates} $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$. 
970 Closed subsets of $X=K^n$ and of $T^*X=K^n\times(K^n)^*$ are the sets $V_K(J)$
971 (Definition~\ref{def:spec}) for ideals $J\subseteq K[x]$, resp.\
972 $J\subseteq K[x,\xi]$; by Theorem~\ref{thm:nss}(a) they are the
973 closed points of the corresponding $V(J)$. As in Definition \ref{def:cotangent}, every fibre $T_yX$ is identified with $(K^n)^*.$
974 Via the dual coordinates $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n$ we regard
975 polynomials $P\in K[\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n]$ as functions on $(K^n)^*$; in
976 particular $P(1,0,\dots,0)=P(dx_1)$.
977 
978 We write $H:=V_K(x_1)=\{(0,y_2,\dots,y_n)\}\subseteq X$ for the hyperplane on
979 which the $x_1$-coordinate vanishes; then $\pi^{-1}(H)=H\times (K^n)^* = \{(y,\xi)\in T^*X:y_1 = 0\}\subseteq T^*X$.
980 The covector $dx_1$, i.e.\ $\xi=(1,0,\dots,0)$, is conormal to $H$: it vanishes
981 on every direction tangent to $H$.
982 
983 The projective space $\mathbb P^n$ consists of nonzero vectors
984 $(q_0,\dots,q_n)\in K^{n+1}$ taken up to a common nonzero scalar (\emph{homogeneous
985 coordinates}); we embed $X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^n$ by $x\mapsto(1,x_1,\dots,x_n)$. A
986 point with $q_0\neq0$ has \emph{affine coordinates} $x_i=q_i/q_0$, and the points with
987 $q_0=0$ form the hyperplane at infinity. For a vector $q\in K^{n+1}$ representing a point
988 of $\mathbb P^n$ we call its entry $i$ its $i$-coordinate, entry $0$ being the
989 homogenizing coordinate. The \emph{affine part} of a row vector
990 $\ell=(\ell_0,\dots,\ell_n)$ is $(\ell_1,\dots,\ell_n)$.
991 \begin{definition}[{\cite[VIII.1, V]{lang}}]\label{def:fieldextension}
992 If $K\subseteq L$ are fields, we call $L$ an extension of $K$ and write $L/K$. For $z\in L$ and $B\subseteq L$ we say:
993 \begin{enumerate}
994 \item $z$ is \emph{algebraic over $K$} if there exists a nonzero polynomial $g$
995 in one variable over $K$ with $g(z)=0$
996 \item $z$ is \emph{transcendental over $K$} if no such polynomial exists, i.e.\
997 $g(z)=0\Rightarrow g=0$
998 \item The \emph{minimal polynomial} of an algebraic $z$ over $K$ is the polynomial of
999 least degree over $K$ with $g(z)=0$ and coefficient 1 for the highest degree term.
1000 \item $\kappa/K$ is \emph{algebraic} if every element of $\kappa$ is algebraic over $K$
1001 \item $\kappa/K$ is \emph{finitely generated} if $\kappa=K(z_1,\dots,z_m)$ for
1002   finitely many $z_i$, and \emph{separable} if every $z\in\kappa$ that is algebraic over
1003   $K$ has a minimal polynomial without repeated roots - in characteristic zero every
1004   algebraic extension is separable.
1005 \item $B$ is \emph{algebraically independent} over $K$ if
1006 no nonzero polynomial over $K$ in finitely many elements of $B$ vanishes.
1007 \item A maximal algebraically independent subset is a \emph{transcendence basis} of
1008 $L/K$. If $B$ is a transcendence basis, then $L$ is algebraic over the subfield
1009 $K(B)$ generated by $K$ and $B$. 
1010 \item Any two transcendence bases of $L/K$ have the
1011 same cardinality, called the \emph{transcendence degree} $\operatorname{trdeg}_KL$.
1012 \end{enumerate}
1013 The elements of $\kappa$ that are algebraic over $K$ form a subfield, and every
1014 finite set generating $\kappa$ over $K$ contains a transcendence basis.
1015 \end{definition}

1017 \begin{definition}[{\cite[§25]{matsumura}}]\label{def:omega}
1018 Let $\kappa/K$ be a field extension.
1019 \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
1020 \item A \emph{$K$-derivation} of $\kappa$ is a $K$-linear map
1021 $D\colon\kappa\to\kappa$ with $D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a)$.
1022 \item Let $\mathcal G$ be the free $\kappa$-vector space with basis symbols
1023 $\langle a\rangle$, $a\in\kappa$. The space of \emph{differentials}
1024 $\Omega_{\kappa/K}$ is the quotient of $\mathcal G$ by the subspace spanned by
1025 \[
1026  \langle a+b\rangle-\langle a\rangle-\langle b\rangle,\quad
1027  \langle ab\rangle-a\langle b\rangle-b\langle a\rangle,\quad
1028  \langle c\rangle\qquad(a,b\in\kappa,\ c\in K),
1029 \]
1030 and $da\in\Omega_{\kappa/K}$ denotes the class of $\langle a\rangle$.
1031 \item A $K$-derivation $D$ induces the $\kappa$-linear map
1032 $\Omega_{\kappa/K}\to\kappa$, $da\mapsto D(a)$, since the linear map
1033 $\langle a\rangle\mapsto D(a)$ on $\mathcal G$ kills the relations. Conversely, a
1034 $\kappa$-linear map $\Omega_{\kappa/K}\to\kappa$ gives the $K$-derivation
1035 $a\mapsto(\text{its value on }da)$. Hence a basis $d\beta_1,\dots,d\beta_d$ of
1036 $\Omega_{\kappa/K}$ has dual derivations $D_1,\dots,D_d$ with
1037 $D_k(\beta_l)=\delta_{kl}$.
1038 \end{enumerate}
1039 \end{definition}
1040 \begin{definition}[{\cite[§10-§11]{matsumura}}]\label{def:valuation}
1041 Let $F$ be a field. 
1042 \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
1043 \item A subring $W\subseteq F$ is a \emph{valuation ring} of $F$ if for
1044 every $a\in F^\times$ we have $a\in W$ or $a^{-1}\in W$. It is a local ring with
1045 maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_W=W\setminus W^\times$. 
1046 \item A \emph{discrete valuation ring} (DVR) is a Noetherian valuation ring $V\ne F$. Then $\mathfrak m_V$ is generated by some $\varpi\in V$, and every $f\in F^\times$ is $f=\varpi^ku$ with $k\in\mathbb Z$
1047 and $u\in V^\times$, and $\operatorname{ord}_V(f):=k$. 
1048 \item For a field $\kappa = V/\mathfrak m_V$ we write $\kappa[[t]]$ for the ring of formal power series, and $\kappa((t))$ for its fraction field (formal Laurent series).
1049 \end{enumerate}
1050 \end{definition}
1051 \begin{definition}\label{def:conormal}
1052 We say
1053 \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
1054 \item A closed set $Y$ is \emph{irreducible} if it is not a union of two proper closed subsets, equivalently if $I(Y)$ is prime \cite[Cor I.1.4]{hartshorne}. 
1055 \item For irreducible closed $Y$, the \emph{smooth locus} $Y_{\mathrm{sm}}$ is the set of $y\in Y$ at which the Jacobian matrix of generators of $I(Y)$ has rank $n-\dim Y$, i.e.\ the points where $Y$ has a well-defined tangent space of the expected dimension $\dim Y$ (i.e. without kinks etc.). It is open and dense in $Y$. \cite[Ch. I.5]{hartshorne} 
1056 \item For $y\in Y$ the \emph{conormal space} is
1057 \[N^*_yY=\operatorname{span}_K\{\,df_y : f\in I(Y)\,\}\subseteq(K^n)^*,\qquad
1058  df_y=\textstyle\sum_i\partial_if(y)\,dx_i,\]
1059  \item $N(Y)=\bigcup_{y\in Y_{\mathrm{sm}}}N^*_yY\subseteq (K^n)^*$ denotes the \emph{conormal
1060 cone}. 
1061 \end{enumerate}
1062 \end{definition}

1068  \subseteq\mathbb P((K^n)^*),\]
1069 the Zariski closure of the projectivization of $N(Y)$.

1074 fibrewise scaling $(y,\xi)\mapsto(y,t\xi)$ for all $t\in K^\times$.
1075 \end{definition}

1104 \subsection{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:asymptotic-conormal-formal}}\label{sub:asym}
1105 \begin{proof}
1106 
1107 If $Y$ is nonempty, closed and irreducible, then $\mathfrak p:=I(Y)$ is a prime
1108 ideal and $Y=V_K(\mathfrak p)$. We will write $R=K[x]/\mathfrak p$, $F=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ for the field of fractions, and $\bar x_i$ for the image of $x_i$ in $R$. \\
1109 
1110 If $\bar x_1$ is algebraic over $K$, there exists a nonzero polynomial $g$ over $K$
1111 in one variable with $g(\bar x_1)=0$ (Def. \ref{def:fieldextension}), therefore \ $g(x_1)\in\mathfrak p$. Since $K$
1112 is algebraically closed, $g(x_1)=\lambda\prod_i(x_1-c_i)$ with
1113 $\lambda\in K^\times$, and as $\mathfrak p$ is prime, some factor $x_1-c$ lies
1114 in $\mathfrak p$. Thus $x_1=c$ on $Y=V_K(\mathfrak p)$, and since $Y\cap H = \varnothing$ and $Y\neq\varnothing$, we have $c\neq0$.
1115 In this case $f=x_1-c$ lies in $\mathfrak p=I(Y)$ and has the differential
1116 $df_y=dx_1$ at every point $y\in Y_{\mathrm{sm}}$, so $dx_1\in N_y^*Y$, and its coordinate vector in the dual basis $(1,0,\dots , 0) \in N(Y)$. Every polynomial vanishing on $N(Y)$ therefore vanishes at $(1,0,\dots , 0)$, and
1117 $[dx_1]\in\operatorname{Dir}(Y)$. So we may assume from now on that
1118 $\bar x_1$ is transcendental over $K$.\\
1119 
1120 The rest of the proof follows this rough logic. We construct a discrete valuation
1121 ring $V$ of $F$ at infinity in which $x_1$ vanishes, and the corresponding
1122 generic point $y\in\kappa((t))^n$ of $Y$. The affine cone $\mathcal C$ of $Y$ over the projective tangent plane at
1123 $y$ has a basis $M$ of power series in $t$ that remains a basis at $t=0$, and
1124 whose reduction $M(0)$ has vanishing $1$-coordinate. Hence the row vector
1125 $u=(0,1,0,\dots,0)$, which reads off the $1$-coordinate, satisfies
1126 $uM(0)=0$, and we lift it to row vectors $\ell(t)$ annihilating $\mathcal C$,
1127 with $\ell(0)=u$. By construction the affine part $\tilde\ell(t)$ consists of
1128 conormal covectors to $Y$ at $y$, and $\tilde\ell(0)=dx_1$. A polynomial
1129 vanishing on $N(Y)$ therefore vanishes at $dx_1$, and so $[dx_1]\in\operatorname{Dir}(Y)$.
1130 %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
1131 
1132 We set $\bar x_0:=1$.
1133 
1134 \begin{lemma}\label{lem:W}
1135 Let $\bar x_1$ be transcendental over $K$. There exist a valuation ring $W$ with
1136 $K\subseteq W\subset F$, $\bar x_1\in\mathfrak m_W$, and an
1137 index $j$ with $\bar x_i/\bar x_j\in W$ for all $0\le i\le n$.
1138 \end{lemma}
1139 \begin{proof}
1140 ince $\bar x_1$ is transcendental over $K$, $S=K[\bar x_1]$ is a polynomial
1141 ring, and $\mathfrak q=\bar x_1S$ is a prime ideal of $S$. The localization
1142 $S_{\mathfrak q}\subseteq F$ is a local ring with maximal ideal
1143 $\bar x_1S_{\mathfrak q}$. By \cite[Tag~00IA]{Stacks} there exists a valuation ring
1144 $W$ of $F$ dominating $S_{\mathfrak q}$, i.e.\ $S_{\mathfrak q}\subseteq W$ and
1145 $\mathfrak m_{S_{\mathfrak q}}=\mathfrak m_W\cap S_{\mathfrak q}$. Hence $K\subseteq W$ and
1146 $\bar x_1\in\mathfrak m_{S_{\mathfrak q}}\subseteq\mathfrak m_W$. For nonzero $a,b\in F$ either $a/b\in W$ or $b/a\in W$,
1147 so among the finitely many nonzero $\bar x_i$ we can choose $j$ with
1148 $\bar x_i/\bar x_j\in W$ for all $i$ (note $\bar x_0=1\ne0$).
1149 \end{proof}
1150 
1151 Let $W$ be the valuation ring of Lemma \ref{lem:W}. We define the column vector $q$ in homogeneous coordinates by
1152 \[ q_i:=\bar x_i/\bar x_j\qquad(0\le i\le n),\]
1153 so that $q_i\in W$, $q_j=1$, $q_0=\bar x_j^{-1}\neq0$, $\bar x_i=q_i/q_0$ and
1154 $q_1=q_0\bar x_1$.
1155 
1156 \begin{lemma}\label{lem:place}
1157 For $q$ defined as above, there exists a discrete valuation ring $V$ with $K\subseteq V\subset F$,
1158 $\operatorname{Frac}V=F$, such that, with $\kappa=V/\mathfrak m_V$ and $\operatorname{res}(q_i)$ the
1159 residue of $q_i$ in $\kappa$:
1160 \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
1161 \item $q_i\in V$ for all $i$, $\bar x_1\in\mathfrak m_V$ and $R\not\subseteq V$;
1162 \item $\kappa$ is algebraic over $L=K(\operatorname{res}(q_0),\dots, \operatorname{res}(q_n))$;
1163 \item $\Omega_{V/K}$ is a finitely generated $V$-module.
1164 \end{enumerate}
1165 \end{lemma}
1166 \begin{proof}
1167 Let $C_0=K[q_0,\dots,q_n,\bar x_1]\subseteq W$; it is finitely generated with
1168 $\operatorname{Frac}C_0=F$ since on one hand $\bar x_i = q_i/q_0$ so every $\bar x_i$ is in $\operatorname{Frac}C_0$ and on the other hand, $F$ is generated by the $\bar x_i$. Let $A$ be its integral closure in $F$. Since $C_0$ is a domain finitely generated over a field, $A$ is a finite $C_0$-module \cite[Thm. I.3.9A]{hartshorne}, hence a finitely generated, integrally
1169 closed Noetherian domain with $\operatorname{Frac}A=F$ and $q_i\in A$. As $W$ is a valuation ring and thereby is
1170 integrally closed \cite[Prop 5.18]{atiyah}, $A\subseteq W$. $\bar x_1 \in \mathfrak m_W$, so $x_1$ is a nonzero nonunit of $W$ and therefore also of $A$. Since $\bar x_1$ is a nonzero nonunit of the Noetherian domain $A$, the ideal
1171 $\bar x_1A$ is proper, so some prime $\mathfrak p_D$ is minimal among the primes
1172 containing $\bar x_1$. By Krull's principal ideal theorem
1173 \cite[Corr. 11.17]{atiyah}, $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p_D\le1$. Because $A$
1174 is a domain, the only prime of height $0$ is $(0)$, and $\bar x_1\neq0$ lies in
1175 $\mathfrak p_D$; hence $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p_D=1$.
1176 
1177 Put $V:=A_{\mathfrak p_D}$. This is a Noetherian local domain. It is normal,
1178 since a localization of an integrally closed domain is integrally closed \cite[Prop 5.12]{atiyah}, and
1179 its dimension equals $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p_D=1$. A Noetherian local
1180 normal domain of dimension one is a discrete valuation ring
1181 \cite[Thm. 11.2]{matsumura}, so $V$ is a discrete valuation ring.
1182 
1183 It remains to check the listed properties.
1184 \begin{itemize}
1185 \item $K\subseteq A\subseteq V$: we have $K\subseteq C_0\subseteq A$, and the
1186 domain $A$ embeds into its localization $V$.
1187 \item $\operatorname{Frac}V=F$: since $A\subseteq V\subseteq F$ and
1188 $\operatorname{Frac}A=F$.

1317 \[\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p^2\to\Omega_{K[x]/K}\otimes_{K[x]}R\to\Omega_{R/K}\to0 ,\]
1318 where $\Omega_{K[x]/K}$ is free on $dx_1,\dots,dx_n$, so the middle term is $R^n$
1319 and the first map sends $f$ to $df(\bar x)$. Hence
1320 $\Omega_{R/K}=R^n/\operatorname{span}_R\{df(\bar x):f\in\mathfrak p\}$.

1322 $S=R\smallsetminus\{0\}$ $\Omega_{F/K}=F\otimes_R\Omega_{R/K}$ and since localization is exact, applying $F\otimes_R-$ to the description of
1323 $\Omega_{R/K}$ above gives
1324 \[ \Omega_{F/K}=F^n/\operatorname{span}_F\{df(\bar x):f\in\mathfrak p\}.\]

1442 Suppose we have a \textit{nonempty} closed subset $W\subseteq T^*X$ that is fibre-conical and involutive. Suppose further, that $W\cap\pi^{-1}(H)=\varnothing$. 
1443 
1444 We will give a proof by contradiction, with this rough logic: Suppose $W\neq\varnothing$ and let $Y = \pi(W)$. Since $W$ is by assumption fibre-conical, we start by showing that $Y\times \{0\} = \pi(W)\times \{0\} \subseteq W$ and $Y \cap H = \varnothing$.  Using involutivity, we then show that we can translate along $W$ in the direction of the fibres which means that all conormal covectors of every irreducible component $Y_0$ of $Y$ lie in $W$. This means $P$ vanishes on all of $W$, hence on $N(Y_0)$. This gives us the setup of Theorem \ref{thm:asymptotic-conormal-formal}, which implies $P(1,0,\dots,0) = 0,$ a contradiction to our assumption. \\
1445 
1446 
1447 We define the (radical) vanishing ideal $J=I(W)\subseteq K[x,\xi]$ consisting of all polynomials that vanish at every point $(y,\xi)\in W$, and $B=J\cap K[x]$, consisting of the functions $f(x)$ with $f(y)=0$ for every $(y,\xi) \in W$. \\
1448 Because $W$ is fibre-conical, the $\xi$-homogeneous components of any
1449 polynomial in $J$ again lie in $J$: if $g=\sum_dg_d\in J$ and
1450 $(y,\xi)\in W$, then $\sum_da^dg_d(y,\xi)=g(y,a\xi)=0$ for all $a\in K^\times$,
1451 and a polynomial in $a$ with infinitely many roots is zero, so every
1452 $g_d(y,\xi)=0$ and therefore $g_d \in J$. 
1453 Let $g \in J$ be written in its $\xi$-homogeneous components form. In particular, $g_0 \in J$. Since $g_0$ has $\xi$-degree $0$, it is a polynomial in $x$ alone, so $g_0 \in B$. Let $Y = V_K(B)$. For $y \in Y$ we therefore have $g(y, 0) = g_0(y) = 0$, so every $g \in J$ vanishes at $(y,0)$, i.e. $(y,0)\in V_K(J)$. $V_K(J) = V_K(I(W)) = W$, since $W$ is closed \cite[Prop. I.1.2]{hartshorne},  hence $(y, 0) \in W$, or $Y \times \{0\} \subseteq W$. 
1454 Furthermore, $Y$ is the projection of $W$ and is therefore per definition of $Y$ closed: $\pi(W)\subseteq Y$ since for ($y',\xi')\in W$ and $f \in B\subseteq J$, we have $f(y') = f(y',\xi') = 0$ so $y' \in V_K(B) = Y$, and $Y\subseteq \pi(W)$, since if $y \in Y$, then also $(y,0)\in W$, so $y = \pi(y,0) \in \pi(W)$.\\
1455 Lastly, assume there exists a $y \in Y\cap H$, then $(y,0)\in W$ and $\pi(y,0) = y \in H$, which would put $(y,0)\in W\cap \pi^{-1}(H)$, which is wrong by our assumption. Therefore, $Y \cap H = \varnothing$.\\
1456 
1457 For the next argument, we also refer to \cite[Ch. E.2]{HTT}. Let $f\in B$. Then $f\circ\pi\in J$.
1458 The Hamiltonian derivation of $f$ applied to a $a \in J$ is
1459 \[ H_f g=\sum_i\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}
1460  \frac{\partial g}{\partial\xi_i} = \{f,g\}\]
1461 since the second term of the Poisson bracket vanishes because $f$ does not depend on $\xi$. Since $f, g \in J$ and $\{J, J\} \subseteq J$ by the involutivity of $W$, it follows that $H_f g \in J$ and by induction also $H^m_fg \in J$. 
1462 For a fixed $y \in Y$ and $g \in J$, we define the polynomial $\varphi(t)=g(y,t\,df_y)\in K[t]$. Its derivative is $\varphi'(t) = \sum_i\partial_{x_i}f(y)\frac{\partial g}{\partial \xi_i}{(y, tdf_y)} = H_fg(y, tdf_y)$, so by iteration, $\varphi^{(m)}(t) = H^m_fg(y, tdf_y)$ for $m\geq 0.$ 
1463 Evaluating at $t = 0$, $\varphi^{(m)}(0) = H_f^mg(y,0).$ Since we have established that $(y,0)\in W$ and $H^m_fg \in J$, and all polynomials in $J$ vanish on $W$, we have $\varphi^{(m)}(0) = 0$ for all $m\geq 0$ -- so $\varphi(t)$ is a polynomial it $t$ all of whose derivatives vanish at $t = 0$.  If we write $\varphi(t) = \sum_m c_mt^m$, then $\varphi^{(m)}(0) = m!c_m$ and since $m!\neq 0$ because $K$ has characteristic zero, $c_m = 0$ for all $m$, and therefore, $\varphi(t) = 0$ everywhere. Remembering the definition of $\phi = g(y,t\,df_y) = 0$, so $(y,t\,df_y) \in V_K(J) = W$.
1464 More generally, since any linear combination of elements in $B$ is again in $B$, we can apply this result to $f = \sum_a c_af_a \in B$ and evaluating at $t = 1$, it follows that 
1465 \[(y, df_y) = (y, d(\sum_ac_af_a)_y) = (y, \sum_a c_ad(f_a)_y) \in W,\]
1466 so the whole linear span $\{y\}\times \operatorname{span}\{df_y:f \in B\}$ is a subset of $W$.\\
1467 
1468 
1469 Since $V_K(B)=Y=\pi(W)\neq\varnothing$, $B \neq K[x]$, so $B$ must have at least one minimal prime. The ideal $B$ is radical (by construction since it is a vanishing ideal) in the Noetherian ring $K[x]$, so $B$ has only finitely many minimal primes $\mathfrak q_1,\dots,\mathfrak q_r$ and $B=\bigcap_i\mathfrak q_i$ \cite[Cor. I.1.4 and Prop. I.1.5]{hartshorne}. We choose $\mathfrak q=\mathfrak q_1$ and set $Y_0=V_K(\mathfrak q)$. Since $\mathfrak q$ is prime, $1\notin\mathfrak q$, so $Y_0\neq\varnothing$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:nss}; moreover $Y_0$ is closed and irreducible since $\mathfrak q$ is prime \cite[Cor.~I.1.4]{hartshorne}, and since primeness implies $\mathfrak q$ is radical, $\mathfrak q = I(Y_0)$ by Theorem \ref{thm:nss}. Additionally, $Y_0\subseteq Y$, hence
1470 $Y_0\cap H=\varnothing$, as we have already established $Y\cap H=\varnothing$.
1471 
1472 Furthermore, if $r = 1$, we choose $s = 1$, then $sh \in \mathfrak q = B$, and if $r\geq 2$, choose for every $i\geq 2$ elemens $s_i \in \mathfrak q_i \setminus \mathfrak q$. Then $s = s_2s_3\dots s_r$ does not lie in $\mathfrak q$ since no factor lies in $\mathfrak q,$ but does lie in every other $\mathfrak q_i$ since they are ideals and one of the factors lies in them. On the other hand, if $h \in \mathfrak q$, then $sh \in \mathfrak q$ since $h\in \mathfrak q$, and $sh \in \mathfrak q_i$ since $s \in \mathfrak q_i$ for all $i\geq 2$. Therefore, $sh \in \bigcap_i \mathfrak q_i = B,$ i.e. $s\mathfrak q \subseteq B.$ We now fix such a $h \in \mathfrak q.$ For a $y \in Y_0\cap\{s\neq0\}$, $(y, dh_y) \in W:$
1473 Since $sh \in B,$ and $d(sh)_y = s(y)dh_y+h(y)ds_y = s(y)dh_y$ since $h(y) = 0$, so $(y, s(y)dh_y) \in W$ and since $s(y)\neq 0,$ also $(y, dh_y)\in W$. 
1474 For $g\in J$, the polynomial $G(x)=g(x,dh_x)$ therefore vanishes on $Y_0\cap\{s\neq0\}$, and $sG$ vanishes on all of $Y_0$, so $sG \in \mathfrak q$ and therefore $G \in \mathfrak q$, which means that $G$ vanishes on all of $Y_0$. Since this is true for every $g \in J$, we have $g(y, dh_y = G(y) = 0$ for all $g\in J$, so $(y,dh_y) \in V_K(J)= W.$  As before, we can argue that since $\mathfrak q$ is an ideal, it is closed under $K$-linear combinations, so for $h_1,\dots ,h_k \in \mathfrak q$ and $c_a \in K$, also $(y, \sum_a c_a (dh_a)_y) \in W$, or put differently, $\{y\} \times \operatorname{span}\{dh_y: h \in \mathfrak q\} \subseteq W$ for all $y \in Y_0$. At a smooth point $y$ of $Y_0$, by Definition \ref{def:conormal}, the conormal space is 
1475 \[N_y^*Y_0 = \operatorname{span}\{df_y : f \in \mathfrak q\}\]
1476 and we have already established that $\mathfrak q = I(Y_0)$. We therefore have that $\{y\} \times N_y^*Y_0 \subseteq  W$ for every smooth $y \in Y_0$, so $P$, which only depends on $\xi$, vanishes on $N(Y_0)$. Because we have established, that $Y_0$ is closed, irreducible, and $Y_0 \cap H = \varnothing $, $Y_0$ therefore fulfills all requirements of Theorem \ref{thm:asymptotic-conormal-formal}, so $P(1,0,\dots,0) = 0,$ contradicting our initial assumption. Therefore, $W$ must be empty.
1477 \end{proof}

1509 \begin{lemma}\label{lem:base-change}
1510 Let $K/k$ be a field extension and put
1511 $\mathcal I_K=\tilde dA_n(K)+x_1^N\tilde dA_n(K)$, $Q_K=Q\otimes_kK=A_n(K)/\mathcal I_K$, with the
1512 order filtration $Q_{K,i}=q\,F_iA_n(K)$. Then
1513 \[
1514  \gr Q_K\cong\gr Q\otimes_kK,\qquad
1515  \Ann_{K[x,\xi]}\gr Q_K=\bigl(\Ann_{k[x,\xi]}\gr Q\bigr)\,K[x,\xi],
1516 \]
1517 and $Q=0\iff Q_K=0$. In particular, $\Char(Q_K)$ is the preimage of $\Char(Q)$
1518 under $\Spec K[x,\xi]\to\Spec k[x,\xi]$.
1519 \end{lemma}
1520 \begin{proof}
1521 By the PBW basis \cite[Prop 1.2.1]{coutinho}, $F_iA_n(K)=F_iA_n(k)\otimes_kK$ and $\mathcal I_K=\mathcal I\otimes_kK$, so
1522 $Q_{K,i}=Q_i\otimes_kK$. As $K$ is flat over $k$,
1523 $Q_{K,i}/Q_{K,i-1}=(Q_i/Q_{i-1})\otimes_kK$, and summing over $i$ gives
1524 $\gr Q_K=\gr Q\otimes_kK$. The module $\gr Q$ is finitely generated (cyclic,
1525 generated by the class of $q$) over the Noetherian ring $k[x,\xi]$, and
1526 $k[x,\xi]\to K[x,\xi]$ is flat, so the annihilator of the finite module
1527 commutes with this base change \cite[Tag~07T8]{Stacks}. Finally, $Q\otimes_kK=0$
1528 implies $Q=0$, because $K$ is a nonzero free $k$-module.
1529 \end{proof}
1530 
1531 Let $d\in A_n(k)$ be nonzero of Bernstein degree $N$ and let
1532 $\tilde d=c^{-1}\Phi_g(d)$ be its monic normal form
1533 (Remark~\ref{rem:transport}). If $n=0$, then $A_0(k)=k$ and every nonzero $d$ is
1534 a unit anyways, so let $n\ge1$. As in Section \ref{sec:euler} we define
1535 \[ \mathcal I=\tilde dA_n(k)+x_1^N\tilde dA_n(k),\qquad Q=A_n(k)/\mathcal I .\]
1536 It suffices to show $Q=0$: then $1\in \mathcal I$, i.e.\ there are
1537 $R,S\in A_n(k)$ with $1=\tilde dR+x_1^N\tilde dS$.
1538 
1539 Let $K=\bar k$, and let
1540 \[W = V_K\bigl(\operatorname{Ann}_{K[x,\xi]}\operatorname{gr}Q_K\bigr)\subseteq K^n\times (K^n)^*,\]
1541 be the set of closed points of $\operatorname{Char}(Q_K)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:nss}),
1542 with vanishing ideal $I(W)=\sqrt{\operatorname{Ann}\operatorname{gr}Q_K}$.
1543 We check
1544 the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:coisotropic-exclusion} for $W$ and the
1545 polynomial $P=\sigma^{\mathrm{\ord}}_N(\tilde d)$ of Section~\ref{sec:monic}:
1546 
1547 \begin{itemize}
1548 \item $W$ is Zariski closed, since it is the zero set of an ideal of polynomials.
1549 \item The module $\gr Q_K$ is graded by the order filtration, which assigns degree
1550 $0$ to the $x_i$ and degree $1$ to the $\xi_i$; so $\Ann\gr Q_K$  is homogeneous in $\xi$. Hence if $(y,\xi)\in W$
1551 and $g\in\Ann\gr Q_K$, then $g(y,t\xi)=\sum_dt^dg_d(y,\xi)=0$ for every
1552 $t\in K^\times$, where $g_d$ denotes the $\xi$-homogeneous component of degree $d$ of $g$. So
1553 $(y,t\xi)\in W$, i.e.\ $W$ is fibre-conical.
1554 \item By Gabber's theorem (Theorem~\ref{ithm:gabber}) $I(W)$ is involutive. 
1555 \item By eq.~\eqref{eq:P-annihilates}, $P\in
1556 \Ann_{k[x,\xi]}\gr Q$, hence $P\in\Ann_{K[x,\xi]}\gr Q_K$ by
1557 Lemma~\ref{lem:base-change}, so $P$ vanishes on $W$.
1558 \item By eq.~\eqref{eq:P-e1}, $P(1,0,\dots,0)=1\neq 0$.
1559 \item  By Lemma~\ref{lem:base-change}, $\operatorname{Char}(Q_K)$ is the preimage of
1560   $\operatorname{Char}(Q)$ under $\operatorname{Spec}K[x,\xi]\to\operatorname{Spec}k[x,\xi]$,
1561   and $V(x_1)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}K[x,\xi]$ is the preimage of
1562   $V(x_1)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}k[x,\xi]$. Since preimages commute with
1563   intersections, Proposition~\ref{prop:char-avoids} gives
1564   $\operatorname{Char}(Q_K)\cap V(x_1)=\emptyset$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:nss}(a) and Definition~\ref{def:spec}, a closed point $\mathfrak m_{(y,\xi)}$ lies in $V(x_1)$ if and only if $y_1=0$, i.e.\ the closed points of $V(x_1)$ form $\pi^{-1}(H)=H\times(K^n)^*$. Hence the closed points of $\operatorname{Char}(Q_K)\cap V(x_1)$ are $W\cap\pi^{-1}(H)$, and so $W\cap\pi^{-1}(H)=\emptyset$.
1565 \end{itemize}
1566 By Theorem~\ref{thm:coisotropic-exclusion}, $W=\varnothing$. As $K$ is
1567 algebraically closed, by Theorem \ref{thm:nss}  $1 \in \Ann_{K[x,\xi]}\gr Q_K$,
1568 so $\gr Q_K=0$. Since the filtration of $Q_K$ is exhaustive with $Q_{K,-1}=0$,
1569 induction on $m$ implies $Q_{K,m}=0$ for all $m$, so $Q_K=0$, and
1570 $Q=0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:base-change}. Therefore, $A_n(k) = \tilde dA_n(k) + x_1^N\tilde dA_n(k)$, or equivalently $1=\tilde dR+x_1^N\tilde dS$
1571 with $R,S\in A_n(k)$.
1572 
1573 Lastly, we apply the inverse of the automorphism $\Phi_g$ of
1574 Remark~\ref{rem:transport}. Since $\tilde d=c^{-1}\Phi_g(d)$ with $c\in k^\times$
1575 central, this gives
1576 \[
1577  1=d\bigl(c^{-1}\Phi_g^{-1}(R)\bigr)+F\,d\bigl(c^{-1}\Phi_g^{-1}(S)\bigr),
1578  \qquad F=\Phi_g^{-1}(x_1)^N,
1579 \]
1580 which proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

1597 Lastly, as Stafford already showed, from $1 = dR+FdS$ follows cyclicity of the torsion right module:
1598 \begin{corollary}[\cite{Stafford1978}]\label{cor:cyclic}
1599 Every finitely generated torsion right $A_n(k)$-module is cyclic.
1600 \end{corollary}
1601 
1602 \begin{proof}
1603     Suppose for every $d\neq 0 \in A_n$, there exists $F, R, S \in A_n$, such that $1 = dR + FdS$. Let us denote a torsion module by $T$. Stafford's theorem (the right module analogue to Theorem 3.7 \mbox{\cite[p.~438]{Stafford1978}}) established, that every finitely generated (right) torsion module can be generated by 2 generators, so let us denote the generators of $T$ with $t_1$ and $t_2$. We can define a homomorphism
1604     \[\phi:A_n \oplus A_n\rightarrow T \quad \phi(r,s)\mapsto t_1r+t_2s.\]
1605     This map is surjective, since $t_1, t_2$ generate all of $T$. By the first isomorphism theorem (for $\varphi:M\rightarrow N$, $im(\varphi) \cong M/ker(\varphi)$), it follows
1606     \[T \cong (A_n\oplus A_n)/M\]
1607     with $M = ker(\phi)$. \\
1608     $T$ is torsion and therefore $\forall t\in T: \exists m \in A_n\backslash\{0\}: tm = 0.$ Specifically, let us denote with $d_1$ and $d_2$ the annihilators of $t_1$ and $t_2$, i.e.
1609     \[t_1d_1 = t_2d_2 = 0.\]
1610     $A_n$ is a right Ore domain \cite[Prop. 1.8.4]{Bjork}, which means it has the property that for any nonzero $d_1, d_2$ there exists elements $r_1, r_2 \in A_n$ such that $d_1r_1 = d_2r_2 \neq 0.$
1611     Defining therefore $d := d_1r_1 = d_2r_2$ yields us a common annihilator of $t_1$ and $t_2$.\\
1612     $(d, 0)$ and $(0,d)$ are elements in $M = ker(\phi)$, since $\phi(d, 0) = t_1d = 0$ and $\phi(0, d) = t_2d = 0.$\\
1613     Because $(0,d), (d, 0) \in M$, also
1614     $(d, 0)S + (0, d)R = (dS, dR) = (dS, 1-FdS) =: (a, 1-Fa) \in M$, i.e. $(a, 1-Fa)$ is an element of $M$. \\
1615     On the other hand, $A_n\oplus A_n$ can be generated by $(a, 1-Fa)$ and $(1, -F)$. We can check this by finding $u,v$ such that for all $(x,y)\in A_n\oplus A_n$ we can write
1616     \[(x,y) = (a, 1-Fa)u + (1, -F)v.\]
1617     Suitable choices are $u = y+Fx$ and $v = x-a(y+Fx)$.\\
1618     Lastly, since  $A_n\oplus A_n$ can be generated by $(a, 1-Fa)$ and $(1, -F)$ and $(a, 1-Fa)$ is an element of $M$, it follows that $T \cong (A_n\oplus A_n)/M$ can be generated by $\phi(1, -F)$, i.e. $T$ is cyclic. \\
1619     Moreover, Stafford defined a module $T$ to be stably cyclic, if, given $l\geq 1$ and $a_1,...a_{l+1} \in T$ that generate $T$, there exist $f_i \in A_n$ such that $(a_i + a_{l+1}f_i)$ $i = 1,...l$ also generate $T$ \cite[p. 435]{Stafford1978}. Since $t_1, t_2$ were arbitrary generators, for $l = 1$ we can set $a_1 = t_1, a_2 = t_2$ and choose $f_1 = -F$, then $\phi(1, -F) = t_1 - t_2F$ generates $T$. For $l\geq 2$ we simply note that by the right module version of \cite[Theorem~3.7]{Stafford1978}, $T$ is already proven to be stably two-generated.
1620     
1621 \end{proof}

1683 \section{AI-assisted research and formalization}\label{app:ai-workflow}
1684 
1685 AI agents assisted with proof discovery, counterexample searches,
1686 review, formalization and writing. The work began with computations in
1687 Macaulay2 and Maple. Direct attempts to construct universal cofactors
1688 produced special cases and reductions, but no general construction. The
1689 decisive step was to study the quotient $Q=A_n/(dA_n+x_1^N dA_n)$ and to show
1690 that its characteristic support is empty. The Lean formalization followed
1691 once the paper argument was complete.
1692 
1693 One controlling agent kept the authoritative state of the proof.
1694 Parallel agents worked on separate approaches and returned evidence together
1695 with its scope, and independent reviewer agents checked each result before it was
1696 accepted. The first author steered the work with short instructions, and the
1697 agents wrote the detailed task prompts for each other. Advice from
1698 consultations counted only after an independent check.
1699 
1700 The working rules developed together with the proof into a reusable
1701 agent skill for open mathematical problems. Early versions emphasized
1702 falsification and keeping conditional results apart from established ones.
1703 Once a candidate proof existed, the emphasis moved to repairing the first
1704 invalid step of a promising argument, and
1705 to auditing proofs.
